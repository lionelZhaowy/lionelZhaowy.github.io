## Project Background
As deep learning models continue to grow rapidly, deployment on resource-constrained edge devices is increasingly limited by compute capacity, memory footprint, and power consumption. Applying a single compression technique—such as pruning or quantization alone—often provides only limited gains before accuracy or hardware-efficiency constraints become dominant.

This project investigated and systematized joint compression paradigms that combine the complementary strengths of pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation, with the objective of achieving a better trade-off between model compactness and predictive accuracy.

## Reflections and Summary
### Personal Evaluation: From Algorithmic Foundations to Architecture Research
This was the first research project that I independently led as an undergraduate. It gave me a rigorous foundation in neural networks and model-compression methods and, together with my background in integrated-circuit engineering, helped define my subsequent research focus on AI accelerators. The project concentrated on reproducing established pruning, quantization, and knowledge-distillation methods rather than proposing a new compression algorithm; its principal scholarly outcome was a structured survey of joint compression techniques. Nevertheless, the resulting theoretical foundation directly informed my later work on FPGA-based AI accelerators and model compression for custom processors.

### Evolution of Compression Algorithms: From the CNN Era to the LLM Epoch
Although pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation emerged during the CNN era, they remain fundamental to model deployment and inference in the large language model (LLM) era. As large-scale models have moved into production, these techniques have evolved from edge-oriented optimizations into essential components of efficient inference on large accelerator clusters, completing the transition from theoretical methods to widely deployed systems.
- *Pruning & Sparsity*:
  - In the CNN era, unstructured pruning attracted considerable attention because it generally preserved accuracy better than coarse-grained alternatives. Its irregular sparse matrices, however, were difficult to accelerate efficiently on general-purpose hardware, motivating greater use of hardware-friendly structured pruning.
  - In the LLM era, the accuracy loss associated with coarse-grained structured pruning is often difficult to recover. This has encouraged hardware–software co-designed semi-structured sparsity. NVIDIA architectures from Ampere onward, for example, support patterns such as 2:4 sparsity to improve compute throughput and memory-access efficiency while retaining model quality.
  - Sparsity has also become a model-level design principle. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, exemplified by DeepSeek models, use dynamic routing to activate only a small subset of parameters for each input. This can be viewed as dynamic, conditional sparsification that reduces the effective cost of inference.
- *Quantization*:
  - During the CNN era, quantization was primarily intended to compress models for deployment on edge or mobile devices with severely constrained compute and power resources.
  - In the LLM era, autoregressive generation often shifts inference from a compute-bound to a memory-bandwidth-bound regime. Quantization has therefore become a principal means of mitigating the memory wall by reducing model storage and data-movement costs. The field has progressed from integer post-training methods such as GPTQ and AWQ to low-precision floating-point computation, including the FP8 formats adopted by DeepSeek-V3 and NVIDIA Blackwell, while preserving competitive model quality.
- *Knowledge Distillation*:
  - In the CNN era, knowledge distillation typically trained a compact student model to reproduce a larger teacher's soft targets or intermediate feature representations, often with white-box access to the teacher.
  - In the LLM era, where many high-capacity teachers are available only through black-box APIs, distillation increasingly transfers reasoning behavior through generated supervision rather than direct feature alignment. Teacher models act as data-generation engines that produce reasoning traces and high-quality instruction data, enabling relatively compact models—for example, 1.5B- or 7B-parameter variants—to outperform earlier models of comparable size.
