## Project Background
As deep learning models continue to grow rapidly, deployment on resource-constrained edge devices is increasingly limited by compute capacity, memory footprint, and power consumption. Applying a single compression technique—such as pruning or quantization alone—often provides only limited gains before accuracy or hardware-efficiency constraints become dominant.

This project investigated and systematized joint compression paradigms that combine the complementary strengths of pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation, with the objective of achieving a better trade-off between model compactness and predictive accuracy.

## Reflections and Summary
### Personal Evaluation: From Algorithmic Foundations to Architecture Research
This was the first research project that I independently led as an undergraduate. It gave me a rigorous foundation in neural networks and model-compression methods and, together with my background in integrated-circuit engineering, helped define my subsequent research focus on AI accelerators. The project concentrated on reproducing established pruning, quantization, and knowledge-distillation methods rather than proposing a new compression algorithm; its principal scholarly outcome was a structured survey of joint compression techniques. Nevertheless, the resulting theoretical foundation directly informed my later work on FPGA-based AI accelerators and model compression for custom processors.

### The Evolution of Compression: From CNNs to Large Models
Pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation remain important optimization techniques for model deployment and inference in the era of large models, while evolving into new forms of hardware–software co-design. Sparsification increasingly emphasizes regular, hardware-friendly computational structures; low-bit quantization has expanded from compressing models for edge devices to reducing the storage, bandwidth, and communication overhead of weights, activations, and caches; and knowledge distillation has evolved from aligning soft labels and intermediate features to leveraging stronger models to generate training data and reasoning traces. Although the specific techniques differ from those used in the CNN era, their fundamental objective remains the same: reducing the storage, computation, and deployment costs of models with minimal accuracy degradation.
