## Project Background
With the exponential growth of deep learning model sizes, deploying them on resource-constrained edge devices faces severe challenges due to compute, storage space, and power consumption bottlenecks. Single compression algorithms (such as pruning-only or quantization-only) often hit performance ceilings prematurely.

This project aimed to overcome the limitations of single algorithms by investigating and systematically reviewing joint compression paradigms that leverage the synergistic advantages of pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation, thereby achieving a superior balance between model compactness and high accuracy.

### Evolution of Compression Algorithms: From the CNN Era to the LLM Epoch
Although pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation originated in the CNN era, they remain indispensable underlying infrastructures for model deployment and inference in today's Large Language Model (LLM) epoch. Accompanied by the boom in deploying large models, these three types of algorithms have evolved from early optimizations targeting edge deployment to the current rigid demands of cloud-based inference on massive GPU clusters, completing the closed loop from theoretical research to industrial application.
- *Pruning & Sparsity*:
  - In the CNN era, unstructured pruning was highly regarded for its ability to maximally preserve model accuracy. However, the resulting irregular sparse matrices were difficult to accelerate on general-purpose hardware, leading the industry to increasingly compromise in favor of structured pruning for better hardware compatibility.
  - Entering the LLM era, however, the accuracy degradation introduced by coarse-grained structured pruning proved difficult to mitigate. Consequently, the industry pivoted towards semi-structured pruning rooted in deep hardware-software co-design. For example, NVIDIA introduced support for 2:4 sparse compute starting from its Ampere architecture, which exponentially boosted computational throughput and memory access efficiency while maintaining accuracy.
  - Meanwhile, the philosophy of sparsity has been elevated within the macroscopic architectures of LLMs. The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, exemplified by DeepSeek, utilizes a dynamic routing mechanism to activate only a small subset of parameters during each inference pass. This inherently constitutes dynamic pruning, pushing the boundaries of model inference efficiency to new extremes.
- *Quantization*:
  - During the CNN era, quantization was primarily intended to compress models for deployment on edge or mobile devices with severely constrained compute and power resources.
  - In the LLM era, however, the autoregressive nature of generation has shifted the inference bottleneck from compute-bound to memory-bound. Quantization has thus emerged as a core strategy to circumvent the "memory wall" by reducing communication overhead. Evolving from early INT schemes like GPTQ and AWQ—which traded compute for VRAM—the field has progressed to the low-bitwidth FP8 formats championed by DeepSeek-V3 and NVIDIA Blackwell. These advancements drastically reduce memory footprint and bandwidth demands while retaining highly competitive model capabilities.
- *Knowledge Distillation*:
  - In the CNN era, the core premise of knowledge distillation was to allow a lightweight student model to emulate the output soft labels or intermediate feature representations of a massive teacher model in a white-box manner.
  - In the LLM era, however, when dealing with massive closed-source large models accessible only via black-box APIs, knowledge distillation has fundamentally shifted from traditional output alignment to cognitive transfer at the reasoning and logic level. Today, teacher models serve as data generation engines. By producing chains of thought and synthesizing high-quality instruction data to train smaller models, relatively lightweight models (e.g., 1.5B or 7B parameters) are now capable of vastly outperforming their same-sized predecessors.
