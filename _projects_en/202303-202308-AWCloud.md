## Project Background
This project addressed a challenge in the 2023 National College Student Integrated Circuit Innovation and Entrepreneurship Competition (AWCloud Cup). The task focused on automated defect inspection for industrial aluminum-can and aluminum-sheet production lines. The organizers provided a dedicated dataset, the AWCloud platform based on an Intel Cyclone V SoC, and a reference AI inference accelerator with its associated toolchain.

The dataset comprises four common defect types found in aluminum products:
- zhen_kong (Pinhole)
- ca_shang (Scratch)
- zang_wu (Dirt/Stain)
- zhe_zhou (Crease/Wrinkle)

The FP32 model, trained in PaddlePaddle, achieved 98.45% mAP. PaddleSlim was then used to perform **joint FPGM pruning and quantization-aware training (QAT)**. ## Deployment and Performance
Joint compression improved inference performance while preserving high detection accuracy. The measured results are summarized below:

| Metric Dimension | Pre-Optimization (Baseline FP32) | Post-Optimization (FPGM Pruning + INT8 Quantization) | Net Gain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Detection Accuracy (mAP)** | 98.45% | 97.05% | Accuracy dropped marginally by 1.4% |
| **Parameter Count** | 100% | 87% | 13% reduction |
| **Accelerator Inference Latency** | 473 ms | 170 ms | 2.78× speedup |
| **End-to-End Latency** | 540 ms | 235 ms | **2.3× throughput improvement** |

Figure 6 shows the deployed system in operation. Given 300×300 RGB inputs, it consistently localizes aluminum-surface defects with accurate bounding boxes, demonstrating the practical balance achieved between detection accuracy and edge-side inference performance.

## Reflections and Summary
### Personal Evaluation: From Theory to Practice
This project translated the joint compression principles studied in the [CAS Innovation Training Program]({{ '/projects/202207-202307-semi/#semi_tag' | relative_url }}) into a deployed system. Because depthwise separable convolution is already highly parameter-efficient, the model proved particularly sensitive to pruning: removing more than 13% of its parameters caused a pronounced loss of accuracy. The reference accelerator also supported only coarse-grained per-tensor quantization, which is poorly matched to the heterogeneous channel-wise distributions of depthwise convolution. Quantization-aware training and subsequent fine-tuning were therefore used to recover most of the resulting accuracy loss.

The competition schedule did not permit a substantial redesign of the reference hardware accelerator, so hardware-efficiency bottlenecks continued to limit the final frame rate. This limitation nevertheless motivated my subsequent research into accelerator microarchitecture and system-level optimization.

### AI Systems: The Constraints of Amdahl's Law
The practical inference efficiency of an AI system is determined not only by a model's operation count and parameter count, but also by how effectively the underlying hardware executes the complete workload. At the system level, Amdahl's law bounds the achievable speedup by the portion of execution that remains serial or inefficient. Meaningful optimization must therefore target the dominant end-to-end bottleneck rather than the compute kernel in isolation.

The accelerator demo provided a direct example: HWC-to-CHW tensor rearrangement was implemented as serial loops on the ARM core, leaving the compute array idle and reducing end-to-end utilization. Two complementary remedies are possible:
- *Software parallelization:* Vectorize the rearrangement kernel with ARM NEON SIMD instructions.
- *Dedicated rearrangement hardware:* Implement a pipelined data-layout conversion module in the FPGA fabric to offload the ARM processor.

Data movement limiting computation is not unique to this platform; it is a central challenge in contemporary AI accelerators. The rapid growth of model parameters and context lengths in the LLM era has made this constraint even more prominent. Consequently, accelerator research has shifted from maximizing peak arithmetic throughput alone toward reducing external-memory traffic and improving on-chip data reuse. Preventing compute arrays from stalling because of data starvation is now a primary objective of AI-system design.
