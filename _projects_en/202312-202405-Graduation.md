## Project Background
A vision chip is a System-on-Chip (SoC) for edge computing that inherently integrates sensing, memory, and computing. It overcomes the bandwidth bottlenecks associated with data transmission and elevates the intelligence level of edge-side image processing. However, due to the stringent power and area constraints in edge scenarios, vision chips are intrinsically limited in computational power and storage capacity, making the direct deployment of conventional object tracking algorithms highly impractical.

This project, which served as my undergraduate thesis, aims to design and implement a single object tracking algorithm tailored to the architectural characteristics of a custom-developed vision chip processor within our research group. It realizes a full-stack workflow encompassing "model design - compression optimization - compilation & deployment."

The processor segment employs a heterogeneous computing architecture, consisting of the following three computing cores:
- **MPU**: A microcontroller based on the MIPS instruction set, responsible for executing the global main function, scheduling data transfers, and controlling the activation/deactivation of the heterogeneous cores.
- **Vector Processor (VP)**: A vector processor based on a custom instruction set, tasked with vector operations and data rearrangement.
- **NN Processor (NNP)**: A custom-developed systolic array accelerator compatible with "Convolution-GEMM" operations ([[Paper]](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1049/ell2.13125)[[pdf]](/images/project/202312-202405-Graduation/Electronics%20Letters%20-%202024%20-%20Wang%20-%20A%20Conv‐GEMM%20reconfigurable%20accelerator%20with%20WS‐RS%20dataflow%20for%20high%20throughput.pdf)), specifically designed to accelerate compute-intensive tasks within neural networks.

  <summary>2. Constraints: Limitations of the Chip Microarchitecture</summary>
  <div class="tech-accordion-content" markdown="1">

Deploying algorithms onto this vision chip necessitates overcoming three primary hardware constraints:
- **Operator Type Limits**: The NNP supports specifically configured convolution operations (as detailed in Table 1) and specific-window pooling operations, but lacks support for activation functions other than ReLU and quantized matrix multiplications. Consequently, the algorithmic design must circumvent or equivalently substitute unsupported operator types.
- **Storage Capacity Limits**: Prioritizing high-efficiency on-chip computation, the chip is not equipped with power-hungry external memory interfaces. All model parameters and intermediate feature maps must reside strictly within a mere 4 MB Global Data Memory (DM) without overflowing.
- **Computational Precision Limits**: The processor utilizes a pure integer W8A8B16 inference dataflow (INT8 for weights and activations, INT16 for biases). The 24-bit accumulator must undergo INT16 truncation prior to multiply-shift re-quantization. The model mandates rigorous quantization to avert severe accuracy collapse induced by data truncation.

  <div class="tech-accordion-content" markdown="1">

#### 3.1. SiamVC Network Architecture
Given that the chip's operator library solely supports pure CNN architectures, this project opted for the classical and efficient Siamese network paradigm. The proposed SiamVC (Siamese Network for Vision Chip) architecture, depicted in Figure 2, comprises two parameter-sharing backbone networks (function $\phi$):
- Template Branch: Inputs a target image $z$ with a resolution of 128×128.
- Tracking Branch: Inputs a search region $x$ with a resolution of 256×256.

The feature maps output by both branches undergo a depthwise cross-correlation operation (function $f$) to generate a similarity score map $s=f(\phi(z),\phi(x))$. This map is subsequently fed into the foreground-background classification branch (function $g_{cls}$) and the coordinate bounding-box regression branch (function $g_{ltrb}$), ultimately yielding the predictions $g_{cls}(s)$ and $g_{ltrb}(s)$.

    <img src="/images/project/202312-202405-Graduation/SiamVC.png" width="80%" alt="Figure 2">
    <br>
    <div style="color: #666; font-size: 14px; padding-top: 5px;">
        <b>Figure 2.</b> The proposed SiamVC network architecture
    </div>
</div>

To conform to the constraints of the target hardware platform, SiamVC incorporates specific tailored designs:
- Adopted a pruned MobileNetV2 as the backbone network to ensure all operators fall within the hardware-supported list.
- Designed an anchor-free tracking head based on depthwise separable convolutions, evading complex post-processing steps like NMS (Non-Maximum Suppression) that are notoriously difficult to accelerate on systolic arrays.
- Equivalently substituted the matrix multiplication operations within the depthwise cross-correlation process with 1×1 convolution operations supported by the NNP.

Evaluations indicated that SiamVC's parameter size is a mere 325 kB, with computational requirements reduced to 0.11 GOPS, thereby fully meeting the hardware deployment standards.

#### 3.2. Knowledge Distillation Based on Output Features
To offset the accuracy degradation stemming from extreme model light-weighting and low-bit quantization, this project introduced an output-feature-based knowledge distillation strategy (illustrated in Figure 3). By constructing a larger-scale teacher network (SiamVC-T), the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between its output features and the predicted features of the student network (SiamVC-S) serves as a soft-label loss to guide the training of the student network.

The total loss function is formulated as a weighted sum of the hard loss (Cross-Entropy and IoU losses) and the soft loss:
- Total Loss: $L_{total}=\alpha L_{hard}+(1-\alpha)L_{soft}$
- Hard Loss: $L_{hard}=L_{CE}+L_{IOU}$
  - Foreground-Background Classification Cross-Entropy: $L_{CE}(p,q)=-\sum p(x)\log (q(x))$
  - Coordinate Regression IoU Loss: $L_{IOU}(A,B)=1-IOU=1-\frac{A\cap B}{A\cup B}$
- Soft Loss: $L_{soft}=L_{MSE_{cls}}+L_{MSE_{loc}}$
  - Feature-Level MSE Loss: $L_{MSE}(G,Y)=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(G_{i}-Y_{i})^{2}$

<details class="tech-accordion" markdown="1">
  <summary>4. Compilation and Deployment Toolchain</summary>
  <div class="tech-accordion-content" markdown="1">

Algorithm deployment relies heavily on the custom compilation toolchain for the vision chip, as shown in Figure 6, encompassing the following core workflows:
- **Model Quantization**: Interfaces with ONNX-format models, executing parsing and PTQ (Post-Training Quantization) to generate operator architectures and parameter files in the chip's customized format.
- **Algorithm-Level Verification**: Implements high-precision modeling of the chip's pure-integer inference dataflow within MATLAB. This assesses the theoretical hardware accuracy in advance and serves as a comparison baseline for back-end VCS simulation verification.
- **Compiler Front-End**: Executes graph optimizations (e.g., operator fusion), node information aggregation, and memory address allocation.
- **Compiler Back-End**: Generates the C-language main function dictating MPU scheduling, alongside configuration register instructions for the NNP computing array.
- **(Optional) VP Assembly**: For unsupported non-linear operators or specific data rearrangement logic, handwritten VP assembly programs are required.
- **RTL Simulation Verification**: Cross-compiles MCU/VP/NNP programs into binaries for VCS simulation, comparing the computational outcomes.
- **Board-Level Testing**: Following successful RTL simulation, the programs are flashed onto the vision chip test board for physical evaluation.

## Reflections and Summary
### Personal Evaluation: From Dedicated Accelerators to Programmable Processors
Compared to the AOD-Net dedicated accelerator I previously helped design in the [Benxin Program]({{ '/projects/202305-202309-microhfut/#micro_tag' | relative_url }}), this project tackled a much more flexible programmable processor. This experience instilled in me a profound realization: whether a processor architecture is "usable" depends heavily on the maturity of its accompanying compilation toolchain. Currently, configuring the custom chip's VP assembly and NNP operators still necessitates substantial manual intervention, significantly raising the development threshold. A comprehensive and user-friendly compiler is not merely a bridge connecting deep learning algorithms to underlying physical computation; it forms the core barrier determining the commercial viability of a chip.

### Hardware-Software Co-Design: Hardware-Aware Compression for Custom Processors
During the algorithmic development targeting our research group's custom architecture, I fundamentally experienced how compression algorithms that perform exceptionally well on general-purpose CPUs and GPUs might suffer from "acclimatization issues" on custom processors. The root cause lies in the far more stringent constraints imposed by custom processors at both the microarchitecture and underlying circuit levels.

Contrasting the inference results on the custom chip versus a GPU, it becomes apparent that the bounding boxes on the custom chip exhibit inter-frame jitter. The root of this issue stems from the fact that the 24-bit accumulator's saturation truncation to INT16—occurring prior to the multiply-shift operation—was not modeled during algorithmic training and quantization, triggering a mismatch in inference results between general-purpose and custom processors.

This phenomenon highlights that even compression
