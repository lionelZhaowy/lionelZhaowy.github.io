## Project Background
While Transformer models exhibit exceptional global modeling capabilities in vision tasks, their deployment at the edge faces severe challenges:
- **Position Encoding Overhead**: To compensate for the permutation invariance of self-attention, positional encodings must be introduced, which imposes additional parameter storage and complex non-linear computational burdens.
- **Compute and Memory Access Bottlenecks**: Global interactions and high-dimensional dense feed-forward networks trigger an explosion in computational and parameter scales, severely limiting the storage capacity, parallel computing power, and energy efficiency ratio of edge devices.

The hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture effectively capitalizes on the complementary structural advantages of both models. It leverages the inherent local receptive fields and translation invariance of CNNs to implicitly extract spatial features, thereby replacing traditional positional encodings and eliminating their derived overhead. Building upon this, it integrates Transformers for global spatial modeling, cutting redundant computation while guaranteeing long-range dependency extraction.

This project represents an architectural evolution from our legacy vision chip ([Undergraduate Thesis]({{ '/projects/202312-202405-graduation/#graduate_tag' | relative_url }})), striving to efficiently support CNN-Transformer models and remediate design flaws present in the first-generation hardware.

  <summary>1. Algorithm Design and QAT Compression</summary>
  <div class="tech-accordion-content" markdown="1">

Based on computational power and accuracy evaluations for edge deployment, MobileViT and EfficientViT were selected as baseline deployment models:
- MobileViT combines inverted residual blocks with Transformer blocks and utilizes an unfold/fold mechanism to convert between feature maps and sequences, thereby encompassing the core operators of hybrid architectures.
- EfficientViT discards high-overhead non-linear operations in favor of a linear-complexity attention mechanism, serving as a paradigm for high-efficiency edge deployment.

The MQBench framework was introduced to refactor the quantization pipeline. By leveraging its computational graph scanning and automated pseudo-quantization node insertion features, we implemented QAT and successfully overcame the severe accuracy degradation associated with the post-training quantization (PTQ) tools in the legacy toolchain. Multi-task heads were mounted on top of the backbones to achieve end-to-end inference across classification, detection, and tracking, as demonstrated in Figure 1.

The compilation and deployment workflow remains similar to the legacy version, taking ONNX models as input. The core phases include:
- **Hardware Modeling and Evaluation**: Constructs a behavioral model to generate the Golden Reference for RTL simulation.
- **Model Compilation and Scheduling**: Parses the computational graph and generates coordinated execution instructions for the MPU, systolic array core, vector core, and reduction core.
- **Verification and Testing**: Upon passing RTL-level simulation verification, instructions are deployed to the hardware platform for physical validation.

### Experimental Evaluation
The hardware-software system proposed in this project was prototyped and verified on a VCU118 FPGA evaluation board. Its performance metrics for the ImageNet classification task under W8A8 quantization are outlined below:

| Model | Top-1 Accuracy (%) | Params | Frame Rate (FPS) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MobileNetv2 | 71.05 (-0.44) | 3.47 M | **498.2** |
| MobileViT-xs | 72.77 (-0.54) | **2.32 M** | 277.1 |
| EfficientViT-B1 | **77.95** (-1.10) | 9.97 M | 363.2 |

The evaluation results indicate that, owing to the hardware-aware QAT strategy and optimized scheduling of the systolic array, the system achieves a high edge inference throughput while fully preserving the high accuracy of the CNN-Transformer models.

## Reflections and Summary
### Personal Evaluation: From CNN to Transformer
In contrast to the legacy chip which exclusively supported CNN architectures, this iterative version enables full support for CNN-Transformer models. Spanning from underlying algorithm selection and quantization compression to custom instruction sets and compiler development, this project allowed me to systematically master the full-stack workflow of DSA hardware-software co-design.

### Architectural Core Evolution: From Compute Power to Storage and Communication
Project implementation validated the law of diminishing marginal utility in computer architecture: aggressively pursuing the peak computing power of execution units is no longer the magic bullet. Instead, the performance bottlenecks of AI chips are conspicuously shifting toward data communication, scheduling, and storage. The following phenomena were explicitly observed in this project:
- **Memory Wall**: Because LayerNorm does not readily accommodate operator fusion like BatchNorm does, it must rely on high-frequency on-chip read/write operations to dynamically calculate means and variances. The data movement latency induced by this non-contiguous memory access introduces overhead that significantly eclipses the mathematical computation of the operator itself.
- **Communication Bottleneck**: Implementing the hardware exponential approximation for SoftMax requires tight coordination between the vector core and the reduction core. The resulting task scheduling and data synchronization overhead across these heterogeneous cores severely impinges upon the overall system performance.

This underscores that the real-world performance ceiling of modern AI chips will be more profoundly dictated by memory hierarchy optimizations and energy-efficient dataflow scheduling mechanisms.
